# Biblical Creation Epistemic Framework

## Introduction

The **Biblical Creation Epistemic Framework (BCEF)** is a structured method for evaluating scientific and historical knowledge claims from the premise that Scripture is true and authoritative, that the biblical creation account is actual history, and that the present creation has approximately 6,000 years of history.

### Framework Summary

BCEF distinguishes revealed fact, direct observation, calibrated measurement, physical interpretation, and historical reconstruction. It treats Creation and other biblically recorded events as real historical boundaries, rejects the conversion of long modeled timescales into actual ages, and compares candidate models by biblical admissibility, empirical compatibility, operational compatibility, explanatory coverage, and assumption cost. The framework preserves observations while exposing the assumptions required to turn present evidence into claims about unobserved history.

### Concise Definition

> **BCEF — Biblical Creation Epistemic Framework:** A systematic method for evaluating knowledge claims about creation by treating Scripture as authoritative revealed history, distinguishing observation from inference and historical reconstruction, and testing scientific models for biblical, temporal, empirical, and operational compatibility.

The central chronological premise is derived from the biblical genealogical and historical record rather than from a single verse: Genesis describes six creation days, Adam is created on Day 6, and the genealogies of Genesis 5 and Genesis 11 provide the principal chronological backbone used by BCEF. Exodus 20:11 independently restates the six-day creation pattern.

The purpose of BCEF is not to re-litigate its premises during every analysis. It asks how physical observations, repeatable science, historical reconstruction, and scientific models should be handled given those premises. Human interpretation remains corrigible even where Scripture itself is treated as true.

The framework also distinguishes God's revealed testimony from human empirical observation. Within BCEF, divine testimony in Scripture is sufficient to establish a scripturally recorded event as historical fact; human eyewitnesses or surviving physical traces are not required for that epistemic status.

## 1. Premises

### 1.1 Scripture is authoritative and true

- BCEF treats Scripture as God's revealed Word and therefore true and authoritative (2 Timothy 3:16-17; John 17:17).
- Events that Scripture presents as historical are accepted as actual history within the framework.
- God's testimony is sufficient; human observation, experimental reproduction, or surviving physical evidence is not required to establish a scripturally recorded event as historical fact.
- Human interpretation of Scripture remains corrigible even though Scripture itself is not.

### 1.2 The present creation has approximately 6,000 years of history

- God created the heavens, the Earth, and their contents in six days (Genesis 1:1-31; Exodus 20:11).
- Adam was created on Day 6 (Genesis 1:26-31).
- BCEF derives an approximately 6,000-year chronology from the biblical genealogical and historical record, with Genesis 5 and Genesis 11 providing the principal genealogical chronology.
- No actual physical history of the present creation may be placed before the creation boundary.
- Therefore, a model that requires millions or billions of years of actual past history is historically incompatible with BCEF, even when the modeled duration is mathematically coherent.

Let:

\[
t_0 = \text{creation}
\]

and let \(t_n\) represent the present. Then BCEF adopts:

\[
t_n - t_0 \approx 6000\ \text{years}.
\]

The numerical total is a framework premise derived from biblical chronology; it is not presented as a number stated verbatim in one biblical verse.

### 1.3 The heavenly lights were created for an Earth-directed purpose

Genesis 1:14-18 assigns the lights in the expanse of the heavens an explicitly Earth-directed purpose: they separate day from night, serve for signs, seasons, days, and years, and give light on the Earth.

BCEF therefore treats the relation of the heavenly lights to Earth as intentional rather than incidental. Their observable light is integral to the purposes stated in the text. BCEF does not begin from a cosmology in which Earth is merely an accidental observation point unrelated to the created purpose of the heavenly lights.

The text does not specify the complete physical mechanism by which all celestial light fulfills that purpose. Mechanistic explanations remain models unless separately established.

### 1.4 Celestial visibility can be temporally ordered

Genesis 1:14 explicitly assigns the heavenly lights a role as **signs**. BCEF therefore does not require every created celestial light to be observable from Earth at every moment in history.

A light may become observable at an appointed time in fulfillment of a created sign-purpose. Matthew 2:1-10 provides an example of a celestial sign associated with a particular historical time. The passage establishes the historically timed sign; it does not specify a photon-propagation mechanism, an emission date, or a distant-light model.

Accordingly, BCEF permits temporally ordered visibility while keeping the physical mechanism open to investigation.

### 1.5 The celestial creation is observationally purposeful but need not be completely visible simultaneously

- Improved light-gathering capability can reveal previously undetected light that is already reaching Earth.
- Present non-detection does not establish non-existence.
- BCEF also leaves open the possibility that additional celestial light becomes observable only when its appointed purpose requires it.
- BCEF therefore does not equate *currently observable* with *all that exists*.
- Genesis 1:14-18 constrains BCEF toward an Earth-directed understanding of the heavenly lights, but it does not explicitly state that no material or spatial extent exists beyond everything humans will ever observe.

The size of any unobservable extension of creation is therefore unresolved unless further biblical or empirical argument establishes it.

### 1.6 The present creation has temporal boundaries

- The present creation has a definite beginning (Genesis 1:1).
- The present heavens and Earth will pass away and be followed by a new heavens and new Earth (2 Peter 3:10-13; Revelation 21:1-5).
- BCEF therefore does not treat the present created order as temporally infinite in either direction.

Let:

\[
C_{present}=[t_0,t_e)
\]

where \(t_e\) is the future termination of the present created order. BCEF does not assign a date to \(t_e\).

The new heavens and new Earth are understood within the framework as a subsequent created order with continued temporal succession and no revealed terminal boundary. The absence of a revealed terminal boundary is not the same claim as timelessness.

## 2. Working Metaphysical Hypothesis

The following is a **working model**, not an explicit statement of Scripture:

> **God timelessly creates the complete finite spacetime history of the present creation as one created order. Genesis describes that order according to terrestrial temporal succession, while Revelation describes its terminal boundary and replacement by the everlasting new creation.**

The wording "timelessly creates" is intentional. Saying God created all time "at the same time" would imply another time outside created time. The hypothesis instead treats God as ontologically prior to created time rather than as an entity located at an earlier instant within it.

This hypothesis does not eliminate temporal sequence inside creation. Events within the created order still occur in genuine succession from the creaturely perspective.

The hypothesis should remain classified as a model unless a proposition is directly established by Scripture.

## 3. Observation and Evidence

The term **observation** must be used carefully because many statements commonly called observations already contain historical assumptions.

### 3.1 Revealed observation / divine testimony

An event explicitly revealed by God in Scripture is accepted as historical fact within this framework. God's testimony is sufficient even when no human observer was present.

Examples include portions of the creation account that no human witnessed.

### 3.2 Human empirical observation

For scientific analysis, human empirical observation should normally mean direct or near-direct data such as:

- instrument readings;
- images;
- counts;
- measured positions;
- spectra;
- laboratory results;
- repeatably observed events.

### 3.3 Calibrated measurement

A calibrated measurement is produced from raw observations using an explicit measurement procedure and calibration model. The calibration assumptions should remain visible when they are material to the conclusion.

### 3.4 Derived physical quantity

A derived physical quantity is calculated from measurements using a physical model. Examples can include inferred mass, distance, temperature, composition, or other quantities that are not themselves raw readings.

### 3.5 Physical interpretation

A physical interpretation explains what observations or derived quantities mean under stated physical assumptions.

### 3.6 Historical reconstruction

A historical reconstruction proposes a sequence of past states or events that is not itself presently observed.

The governing distinction is:

\[
\boxed{\text{measurement} \neq \text{interpretation} \neq \text{historical reconstruction}}
\]

A historical reconstruction must not be promoted to an empirical observation merely because it is widely accepted.

## 4. Operational Science and Historical Science

### 4.1 Operational science has primary research value

Operational science studies how creation behaves under conditions that can presently be observed, measured, tested, and reproduced.

This is the highest-priority scientific domain for the framework because it gives direct access to the behavior of the creation in which we currently live.

### 4.2 Historical science is conditional reconstruction

Historical science attempts to reconstruct unobserved past events from current evidence. It can legitimately use operational science, but every reconstruction depends on assumptions about such matters as:

- initial conditions;
- continuity;
- rates;
- environmental conditions;
- model selection;
- absence or presence of intervening events.

Therefore:

\[
O_{present}+L_{observed}\rightarrow H_{candidate}
\]

rather than:

\[
O_{present}+L_{observed}\rightarrow H_{fact}.
\]

A reconstructed history remains conditional unless independently established.

### 4.3 Biblical history is an exception to experimental reproducibility

A scripturally recorded historical event is accepted as fact even when it cannot be reproduced now.

Therefore:

\[
\text{not reproducible}\not\Rightarrow\text{not historical}.
\]

This includes miracles. A miracle may occur again, but repetition is not required for the original event to be true.

## 5. Creation as the Absolute Origin Boundary

Creation is not merely the earliest point in an indefinitely extendable physical timeline. It is the absolute origin boundary of the present physical creation.

Let:

\[
S(t_0)=S_{created}.
\]

Physics may describe how \(S_{created}\) behaves after creation, but the framework does not require an antecedent physical state:

\[
S(t_0-\Delta t).
\]

There is no created physical history there.

### 5.1 Creation is not accelerated natural formation

A central rule is:

> **Creation is not required to be an accelerated version of a natural process that would otherwise take millions or billions of years.**

If a model says that process \(P\), operating at observed rate \(r\), would require two billion modeled years to produce state \(X\), that statement may be mathematically meaningful as a property of the model. It does not imply that \(X\) actually has a two-billion-year history.

The appropriate conclusion is:

\[
\boxed{\text{That model cannot describe the actual origin of }X\text{ within this framework.}}
\]

There is normally no need to invent a mechanism that compresses the two-billion-year process into 6,000 years. The framework first asks why the proposed process should be assumed to have produced \(X\) at all.

### 5.2 Origin and subsequent behavior are separate questions

A universal methodological rule is:

> **No observed mechanism may be assumed to explain the origin of a created entity merely because it can modify that entity after creation. Origin claims require independent justification and must remain compatible with the biblical creation boundary.**

Formally:

\[
\text{mechanism modifies }X
\not\Rightarrow
\text{mechanism created }X.
\]

and:

\[
\text{law describes }X
\not\Rightarrow
\text{law generated }X.
\]

## 6. Biblical Historical Boundaries

The approximately 6,000-year history is not assumed to be physically uniform from beginning to present. Scripture records major historical events that can establish internal boundaries.

A general representation is:

\[
\text{Creation}
\rightarrow
\text{Created State}
\rightarrow
\text{Fall}
\rightarrow
\text{Post-Fall State}
\rightarrow
\text{Flood}
\rightarrow
\text{Post-Flood State}
\rightarrow
\text{Babel}
\rightarrow
\cdots
\rightarrow
\text{Present}
\rightarrow
\text{Terminal Boundary}
\rightarrow
\text{New Creation}.
\]

The governing rule is:

> **Never transport an observed condition backward across a relevant biblical boundary merely by assuming continuity.**

### 6.1 The Fall

The present world is not assumed to be identical to the original created condition. Genesis 3 records the Fall and curse, and Romans 5:12-21 connects sin and death to Adam.

The framework begins conservatively: consequences directly established or strongly constrained by Scripture are treated as governing constraints. Proposed effects on specific biological, thermodynamic, geological, astronomical, or other physical systems remain models unless independently justified.

### 6.2 The Flood

The Flood is treated as a major historical and environmental boundary. Present environmental conditions cannot simply be projected backward across it.

Genesis records waters above the expanse (Genesis 1:6-8) and, in the Flood account, the fountains of the great deep and the windows of heaven (Genesis 7:11-12; 8:2). A specific atmospheric **water-canopy model** is a model-dependent inference rather than a direct textual statement about atmospheric structure.

The canopy model may be retained as a candidate when useful, but claims about pre-Flood atmospheric pressure, temperature, radiation exposure, or related variables require additional support.

The broader rule remains:

\[
X_{today}\not\Rightarrow X_{pre-Flood}=X_{today}.
\]

### 6.3 Babel

Babel is a scripturally recorded historical boundary (Genesis 11:1-9) relevant to questions involving human language, population dispersal, migration, and related historical reconstructions. It need not be treated as relevant to unrelated physical systems unless a specific argument establishes that relationship.

### 6.4 Miracles

A scripturally recorded miracle is an actual historical event. Science may investigate observable conditions before the event, consequences after it, and physical effects that remain accessible. The divine causal act itself need not reduce to a repeatable natural mechanism.

Thus:

\[
S_{before}\xrightarrow{\text{divine action}}S_{after}
\]

need not be reducible to:

\[
S_{after}=F(S_{before})
\]

under ordinary physical dynamics alone.

## 7. Laws, Constants, Initial Conditions, and Boundary Events

These categories must remain separate.

### 7.1 Operational laws

Physical laws are repeatably observed relationships describing ordinary behavior within tested domains.

They may govern transitions such as:

\[
S_t\rightarrow S_{t+\Delta t}
\]

within their justified domain.

They do not establish that every present state was produced by indefinitely applying those laws backward.

### 7.2 Physical constants

Measured parameters such as \(c\), \(G\), particle masses, and coupling constants are empirical quantities within measurement uncertainty under current accessible conditions.

Claims about their values during another historical period are separate claims requiring evidence. The framework does not require such values to have changed and must not invoke changes merely to rescue an otherwise incompatible model.

For relativity-related discussion, \(c\) should be understood as the invariant causal speed appearing in spacetime structure; writing \(c=1\) is a unit convention, not a separate physical claim.

### 7.3 Created initial conditions

The state established by God's creative action is a legitimate initial condition:

\[
S(t_0)=S_{created}.
\]

It does not require derivation from an earlier natural state.

### 7.4 Boundary events

The Fall, Flood, Babel, miracles, and other scripturally established events can invalidate continuity assumptions for particular domains. Their relevance must be evaluated rather than universally assumed.

## 8. Long-Timescale Models

A proposition requiring millions or billions of years does not describe actual history within this framework.

The following statements must be distinguished:

**Historically inadmissible statement:**

> Process \(P\) operated continuously for two billion years and produced \(X\).

**Permissible conditional model statement:**

> Under model \(M\), process \(P\), operating at rate \(r\), would require approximately two billion modeled years to produce \(X\) from assumed precursor state \(S_0\).

The second can be mathematically valid without being historically relevant.

A long-timescale model has scientific value inside this framework only to the extent that it independently contributes to understanding present behavior, producing a testable result, solving a present problem, or distinguishing between viable models.

A million- or billion-year modeled duration must never be silently converted into an actual age.

## 9. Example: Spiral Galaxies

A present spiral galaxy may be represented as \(G_{now}\).

A conventional historical reconstruction might propose:

\[
G_{precursor}
\xrightarrow[\text{billions of years}]{\text{gravity + gas dynamics + star formation}}
G_{now}.
\]

Within this framework, that proposed history is not required merely because a simulation or theory can produce spiral structure over a long modeled interval.

The actual historical question is instead:

\[
G_{created}
\xrightarrow[\leq6000\text{ years}]{\text{actual subsequent history}}
G_{now}.
\]

The created galaxy may already possess spiral structure, stellar populations, orbital organization, chemical composition, magnetic fields, and other coherent properties. Those may be properties of the created state rather than products of an unobserved pre-creation developmental history.

The primary operational scientific questions concern how such a galaxy behaves now and what changes can reasonably have occurred during its actual history.

## 10. Example: Distant Light and Temporally Ordered Signs

Distant light remains one application of BCEF, but it is not the framework's organizing problem.

Genesis 1:14-18 states that the heavenly lights were created to separate day from night, serve for signs, seasons, days, and years, and give light on the Earth. BCEF therefore treats celestial light at Earth as purposeful rather than incidental.

The sign-purpose adds a temporal constraint: BCEF does not require every celestial light to be visible at every moment. Matthew 2:1-10 provides a biblical example of a celestial sign associated with a specific historical time. The text does not disclose whether such timing involved ordinary propagation, created initial conditions, altered causal geometry, direct divine action, or another mechanism.

Under the approximately 6,000-year chronology, ordinary propagation at the presently measured vacuum causal speed cannot by itself supply an actual multi-million- or multi-billion-year photon travel history. That incompatibility constrains historical models; it does not identify the correct mechanism.

Candidate models can include, without being promoted to premises:

- a created electromagnetic field or created light in transit;
- temporally ordered arrival of light for an appointed purpose;
- synchronization or other non-ordinary initial conditions;
- spacetime or causal-geometry models that alter the relation between coordinate distance and elapsed created history;
- incomplete present understanding of the relevant physics;
- other models that satisfy the biblical chronology and reliable observation.

BCEF does not currently require one of these mechanisms as revealed fact. The correct state may remain unresolved.

The deeper physical issue is causal structure, not visible light alone. In relativity, \(c\) characterizes invariant causal structure, while photons propagate at that causal limit in vacuum.

### 10.1 Origin-history and source-state correspondence

Two questions must be evaluated separately:

- **Q1 — Origin-history:** How long has the arriving light existed as actual physical history?
- **Q2 — Correspondence:** Which state of the source does the arriving information represent?

BCEF's chronology excludes actual multi-million- or multi-billion-year travel histories. It does not establish the exact age of a particular arriving signal or uniquely determine its source-state correspondence. Present distance and present causal speed alone do not settle that history under BCEF's creation boundary. Distance may still be a useful derived spatial quantity; its calibration and model assumptions remain subject to §3.

### 10.2 Day-4 availability and correspondence

Given that light was available on Day 4, it belonged to the actual created condition and had no pre-creation physical history. This is a strong inference (C3) within BCEF.

That claim differs from asserting that every received feature exactly represented the corresponding distant object's state on Day 4. Exact source-state correspondence requires an additional argument and remains model-dependent (C4) unless established. Use **current-state-on-Day-4** only with its intended meaning stated. Day-4 functionality does not establish a census of every source visible then (§§1.3–1.5).

### 10.3 Changing information and created initial conditions

A literally unchanging received Day-4 image cannot account for a changing received record. This rejects the unchanging-image proposal, not created initial light fields generally. A created field could contain an ordered sequence of information.

Observed variation establishes change in the received information. It does not, by itself, establish that this information represents the current state of a distant source today.

### 10.4 Preferred interpretation and retained alternatives

The project owner's preferred interpretation is **current-state-today**: the arriving information represents the current state of those lights today. Its classification remains **C4 — model-dependent inference**. Preference does not establish comparative superiority or completion of the empirical and operational tests in §16.

An authored time-varying record is also retained as a candidate. Authorship describes how a record is established; current-state correspondence describes what it represents. These claims are not necessarily exclusive, and neither exhausts the candidates in §10. A candidate must specify its correspondence assumptions without assigning actual physical events to a pre-creation history.

Causal-geometry models, incomplete physical understanding, and other admissible alternatives remain open. Ordinary propagation during created history is not prohibited; an assumed delay is not an automatic answer to the distant-light problem and must satisfy the chronological and operational gates.

The broad candidates currently have the status **retained candidate; compatibility and comparative ranking unresolved**. Further evaluation should record their assumptions, observational coverage, operational compatibility, and replacement criteria (§14.4). An unknown physical relationship remains an acceptable result.

### 10.5 Detection and the terminal boundary

Improved instruments can detect light already arriving at Earth (§1.5). A new detection alone establishes neither its first arrival nor current-state-today correspondence. Temporally ordered visibility remains permitted; its mechanism is unresolved.

The finite duration of the present creation does not uniquely select a light model. No approximately 2,000-year phase scheme, 7,000–10,000-year total-duration cap, or prediction that the endpoint is near is adopted as a governing premise. The terminal boundary remains undated (§1.6).

## 11. Example: Pre-Flood Environment

The present atmosphere or environment cannot automatically be treated as a direct representation of pre-Flood conditions.

A pre-Flood environmental model is worth developing when it helps:

- explain a present observation;
- understand a scripturally recorded consequence;
- solve a present scientific problem;
- discriminate between competing models.

Otherwise, inaccessible pre-Flood reconstruction has lower research priority.

A water-canopy model is therefore preserved as a candidate model, not promoted to revealed fact.

## 12. Scripture Interpretation Rules

### 12.1 Ordinary meaning is the default

Scripture should normally be understood according to the ordinary grammatical meaning of its words in their immediate and canonical context.

A figurative interpretation requires positive textual or contextual reason. It should not be introduced merely because the ordinary reading conflicts with an external historical model.

### 12.2 Figurative and literal reference are not necessarily exclusive

Figurative significance does not automatically eliminate a literal or historical referent. Symbolic language can refer to real persons, events, places, or future occurrences.

### 12.3 Scripture interprets Scripture

No interpretation should be established in isolation when other Scripture materially constrains its meaning.

Clear and relevant passages constrain interpretations of less clear passages.

### 12.4 Chronology is a controlling constraint

Within this framework, the approximately 6,000-year chronology is a global historical boundary condition. External historical reconstructions cannot be used as controlling evidence to force reinterpretation of an otherwise ordinary biblical historical statement merely because a long-age model requires it.

### 12.5 Observation can correct our interpretation

Scripture is true, but our interpretation may be wrong.

A strong, repeatable observation can therefore require:

\[
\text{observation}\rightarrow\text{reconsider scriptural inference}
\]

without implying:

\[
\text{observation}\rightarrow\text{Scripture is false}.
\]

When an apparent conflict arises, audit both the scientific assumptions and the human interpretation of Scripture.

## 13. Certainty and Claim Classification

The framework should label claims by epistemic status.

### C1 — Revealed fact

Explicitly established by Scripture and accepted as revealed fact within BCEF.

Examples include creation (Genesis 1-2), the Fall (Genesis 3), the Flood (Genesis 6-9), Babel (Genesis 11:1-9), and scripturally recorded miracles. The approximately 6,000-year chronology is a binding BCEF premise derived from the biblical genealogical and historical record (especially Genesis 5 and Genesis 11), rather than a number stated verbatim in one verse.

### C2 — Observed fact

Directly measured, experimentally observed, historically witnessed, or otherwise recorded as observation within actual created history.

C1 and C2 differ by source of knowledge, not by one necessarily being less factual than the other.

### C3 — Strong inference

A conclusion that follows closely from revealed or observed facts and established logical or physical relationships with few substantive assumptions.

### C4 — Model-dependent inference

A conclusion that materially depends on assumptions such as initial conditions, continuity, rate constancy, environmental conditions, model selection, or absence of relevant historical discontinuities.

### C5 — Speculation

A proposition that is logically possible but insufficiently established.

### Historical admissibility is a separate gate

A claim can be mathematically coherent yet historically inadmissible. For example, a simulation may require eight billion modeled years, but an assertion that eight billion years of actual history occurred is excluded by the 6,000-year historical boundary.

## 14. Model Management

Models must remain subordinate to revealed facts, reliable observations, and established operational science.

### 14.1 Preferred-model rule

Prefer the model that:

- satisfies BCEF premises and biblical constraints;
- explains the greatest amount of reliable observation;
- remains consistent with established operational science;
- introduces the fewest unsupported assumptions;
- requires the fewest ad hoc exceptions;
- uses no unnecessary complexity.

"Most easily fits" means the best overall fit under these constraints, not merely the shortest verbal explanation.

### 14.2 Preserve alternatives

Every identified viable model should be recorded even when one is preferred.

The framework must always permit models to be:

- retained;
- merged;
- revised;
- replaced;
- discarded.

A preferred model is not a revealed or observed fact.

### 14.3 Unknown is acceptable

If no adequate physical model is known, the correct state can simply be unresolved.

It is acceptable to say:

> **Scripture establishes that event \(E\) occurred, observation establishes condition \(O\), and the physical relationship between them is not currently known.**

The framework must not invent mechanisms merely to remove an explanatory gap.

### 14.4 Suggested model registry

For recurring analysis, record:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Model ID | Stable reference name |
| Claim addressed | What the model attempts to explain |
| Scriptural constraints | Relevant revealed facts or boundaries |
| Observed data | Measurements the model must account for |
| Assumptions | Non-observed premises required |
| Mechanism | Proposed explanatory structure |
| Strengths | What it explains well |
| Weaknesses | Known unresolved issues |
| Conflicts | Any scriptural or observational tension |
| Status | Preferred, viable, weak, rejected, unresolved |
| Replacement criteria | What would cause revision or rejection |

## 15. Apparent Conflict Procedure

When a conventional scientific conclusion appears incompatible with biblical history, preserve the data and decompose the reasoning.

Record separately:

1. the raw observation or direct measurement;
2. calibration steps;
3. derived physical quantities;
4. physical laws or regularities used;
5. assumed initial conditions;
6. continuity or rate assumptions;
7. intervening historical assumptions;
8. the inferred history;
9. the relevant biblical historical constraint;
10. the precise point at which incompatibility appears.

The governing rule is:

> **Preserve empirical data. Expose every assumption required to convert that data into a statement about unobserved history.**

When a conflict remains unresolved, record it as unresolved. Do not force an explanation.

## 16. Model Admissibility Test

Candidate historical models should pass the following gates in order.

### A. Biblical admissibility

Does the model contradict an explicit scriptural fact or chronology?

If yes, reject it as a description of actual history within the framework.

### B. Temporal admissibility

Does the proposed process fit inside the actual available biblical history and respect relevant boundaries?

If no, reject it as actual history.

### C. Empirical compatibility

Does it account for reliable observations?

If no, revise or reject it.

### D. Operational compatibility

Does it unnecessarily contradict repeatable physical science?

If yes, require specific justification.

### E. Comparative model quality

Only after passing A-D should viable models be compared for simplicity, explanatory coverage, assumptions, and unnecessary complexity.

## 17. Research Priority Rule

The primary research priority is science that can be observed and reproduced now.

Historical reconstruction receives lower priority unless it is needed to:

- explain present evidence;
- understand a scripturally recorded event or consequence;
- solve a present scientific or engineering problem;
- distinguish between competing viable models.

This rule is especially important for inaccessible pre-Flood conditions and hypothetical long-timescale processes.

A speculative reconstruction with no present explanatory, observational, scriptural, or model-discriminating value should be recorded, if useful, but should not consume significant research effort.

## 18. Scientific Consensus and Motive

The framework recognizes that mainstream scientific consensus conflicts with an approximately 6,000-year chronology in major areas such as cosmology, geology, and evolutionary biology.

That establishes a conflict between historical models. It does not establish the motive of every scientist who accepts the mainstream model.

Individual intention can be relevant to sociology or philosophy-of-science analysis, but motive is secondary to evaluating the propositions themselves. Scientific claims should be tested by separating observation, inference, assumptions, and historical reconstruction rather than by assigning motives to individual researchers.

8. The present heavens and Earth have a future terminal boundary (2 Peter 3:10-13; Revelation 21:1-5).
9. The new creation has no revealed terminal boundary (Revelation 21:1-5).

## 20. Claim-Evaluation Procedure

For any scientific proposition, analyze it in the following order:

1. **State the claim precisely.**
2. **Identify relevant scriptural facts and chronology.**
3. **Identify direct observations and repeatable results.**
4. **Separate calibrated measurements from raw observations.**
5. **Identify derived quantities and the models used to obtain them.**
6. **Identify operational laws or regularities invoked.**
7. **List assumed initial conditions.**
8. **List continuity, rate, environmental, and boundary assumptions.**
9. **Determine whether the claim crosses Creation, Fall, Flood, Babel, a miracle, or another relevant biblical boundary.**
10. **Determine whether any proposed actual duration exceeds the biblical temporal domain.**
11. **Classify each conclusion as C1-C5.**
12. **Apply the model-admissibility gates.**
13. **Record viable alternative models.**
14. **Prefer the easiest adequate fit without promoting it to fact.**
15. **Record unresolved conflicts explicitly.**
16. **Ask whether further historical reconstruction has present scientific value before investing in it.**

This procedure is the intended bridge from the foundational framework to specific topics in physics, astronomy, geology, biology, archaeology, and other historical sciences.

## 21. When to Apply
